Design project · Georges Charpak Institute · Sep 2026 — Present

A glenoid implant designed against loosening

In a five-member team working with the Georges Charpak Institute of Human Biomechanics, we study why shoulder implants loosen and propose a glenoid concept, then measure real shoulder motion to feed the models.

01 — The problem

The glenoid side is where implants fail

In total shoulder replacement, loosening of the glenoid component accounts for about a third of complications. The implant sits on a small, thin bone and is loaded off-centre at every movement, which rocks it and breaks the fixation over time.

We reviewed four biomechanical test protocols and five finite-element studies to understand which design choices reduce this rocking.

02 — Concept

Fixation that resists rocking

Our concept, in all-polyethylene:

  • Curved, textured back that matches the bone and spreads the load.
  • Four peripheral pegs (Ø ≈ 4 mm) against rotation and lift-off.
  • Finned, uncemented central peg for bone ingrowth.
  • Rounded edges and 4–6 mm thickness to limit stress peaks.
03 — Validation & kinematics

From concept to evidence

In parallel, we measured shoulder motion with an electromagnetic sensor and a stylus, built anatomical frames for the humerus and scapula, and plotted 90° abduction in Octave. These motions will drive the load cases.

Medical design is mostly about evidence: the concept took two weeks, the plan to prove it will take the rest of the year.
